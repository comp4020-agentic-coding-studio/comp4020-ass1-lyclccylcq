5. Ko and ko threats

Legal moves can still create another problem: two players might capture the same stone forever.

A player cannot immediately make a move that recreates the board position from immediately before the opponent's last move. This is called the ko rule.

The rule doesn't say you have lost the ko — only that you must wait one move. So you play somewhere else first: a ko threat (劫材), a move urgent enough that your opponent has to answer it. Their answer changes the board, and the ko point is yours to take again.

Both players can do this, which is what makes a ko a fight. Below, White's lone stone has just one liberty. Capture it with Black, then take White's side and play the fight out: your threat, Black's answer, your retake — and then Black's threat back at you, and the choice it forces.

Eventually the threats run out and only small, quiet moves are left. How does a game actually end?
